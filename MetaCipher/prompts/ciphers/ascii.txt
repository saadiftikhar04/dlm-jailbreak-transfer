ASCII is a character encoding standard that assigns a unique numeric value to letters, digits, punctuation, and control characters, typically within the range of 0–127. Here is the mapping for letters A through Z:
['a': 97, 'b': 98, 'c': 99, 'd': 100, 'e': 101, 'f': 102, 'g': 103, 'h': 104, 'i': 105, 'j': 106, 'k': 107, 'l': 108, 'm': 109, 'n': 110, 'o': 111, 'p': 112, 'q': 113, 'r': 114, 's': 115, 't': 116, 'u': 117, 'v': 118, 'w': 119, 'x': 120, 'y': 121, 'z': 122]
For example, "104 101 108 108 111" is decrypted to "hello", "109 111 114 115 101" is decrypted to "morse", and "112 121 116 104 111 110" is decrypted to "python".
